Your guide to moving into AI Safety

Four steps into the field, and the first thing to do at each one. The full lists of programmes, grants and reading are there when you want to go deeper.

Your onboarding path

You don't need to quit your job to get started.

Most people who move into AI Safety follow four steps, each of which fits around a full-time job. For each step, start with the highlighted pick; the rest is there when you want more.

  1. 01

    Learn the basics

    From ZAIS

    AI Safety Fundamentals

    A free six-session course at ETH Zurich, introducing the technical foundations of AI Safety research.

    Register your interest

    We also recommend BlueDot Impact's free online Future of AI course.

    More to learn from (3)
    • Technical AI Safety courseBlueDot Impact's next step after the Future of AI course.
    • AI Safety AtlasA free textbook with a structured overview of the whole field, used by many universities.
    • 80,000 Hours' guideHow careers can reduce AI risk.
  2. 02

    Do something small and concrete

    We recommend

    Your first AI Safety contribution in under a week

    BlueDot's step-by-step guide, for example to fixing open issues in open-source safety tools. You don't need deep ML expertise.

    More ways to start (3)
    • Guide for experienced software engineersHow you can scale safety research, build tools for researchers or contribute directly.
    • InspectWrite an evaluation task with the UK AI Security Institute's framework.
    • Apart Research sprintsWeekend research hackathons, open to all.
  3. 03

    Connect with people in the field

    From ZAIS

    Come to an event

    • AI Futures: a 6-part series every two weeks at ETH Zurich.

    See upcoming datesOr book a 1:1 chat

    Apply

    We recommend

All other programmes (12)

Part-time

  • BlueDot Impact: free courses that fit into a few hours a week.
  • Apart Research: weekend research sprints, plus a 3 to 6 month online fellowship.

Bootcamp

  • ARENA: 4 to 5 weeks of ML engineering in London, travel and housing covered.

2 to 3 months, full-time

  • MATS: mentored research, mainly in Berkeley.
  • Pivotal Research Fellowship: 9 weeks in London, extensions up to 6 months.
  • ERA:AI Fellowship: 10 weeks in Cambridge, UK, fully funded, welcomes adjacent fields.
  • GovAI Fellowship: 3 months of AI governance research in London.

4 months or more

  • MATS Residency: 6 to 24 months, with a Bridge track for experienced professionals.
  • Astra Fellowship: fully funded in Berkeley, no prior AI Safety experience needed.
  • Anthropic Fellows Program: four months full-time, based in the US, UK or Canada.
  • OpenAI Safety Fellowship: a pilot with workspace in Berkeley or remote.

For founders

  • Catalyze Impact: incubates new AI Safety organisations, with a phase in London.

Still more: the AISafety.com directory lists over 90 fellowships, courses and bootcamps.

We recommend

BlueDot Impact Career Transition Grants

Fund a defined period of full-time work on moving into AI Safety, usually between $20k and $200k.

Coefficient Giving handed its own career transition funding to BlueDot in September 2026, so this is now the main route for individuals.

What people who made the switch say

Many experienced professionals have moved into AI Safety and written about it. Four lessons come up again and again.

  1. 01

    Build context before you apply

    Skilled professionals often miss how the field works: who does what, and which problems matter. Learn that first, then network.

    Read: Why experienced professionals fail to land high-impact roles

  2. 02

    Offer help, don't only apply

    Find people working on problems you care about and offer them concrete help. It often opens more doors than another application.

    Read: Stop Applying And Get To Work

  3. 03

    Your experience is needed

    The field is short of program managers, operators and other experienced generalists, not only researchers.

    Read: AI Safety's Biggest Talent Gap Isn't Researchers

  4. 04

    Start small, and give it time

    A few hours a week are enough to begin, and the biggest barriers are often psychological. Expect it to take a while: one parent's move from tech took 18 months.

    Read: AI safety starter pack · Tech to AI safety
